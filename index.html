COVID-19 Status and Pathology Results

Background

COVID-19 causes inflammation in the body, which can change the results of routine blood tests. This page compares five of these tests between patients with and without COVID-19.

  • Ferritin - a protein that stores iron and also rises with inflammation.
  • Troponin - a marker of damage to the heart muscle.
  • Neutrophils - white blood cells that respond to infection and inflammation.
  • Lymphocytes - white blood cells involved in the immune response.
  • CRP (C-reactive protein) - a general marker of inflammation.

About the data

  • The data are aggregated: each of the 16 rows represents a group of patients who share the same COVID-19 status, age group, sex and ethnicity. Each row gives the group's average result and its sample size.
  • Every bar is a weighted average, weighted by group size. This is because the groups are very different sizes, and an ordinary average would give small groups too much influence.
  • The bars show the weighted average of one blood marker for patients without COVID-19 and patients with COVID-19.

The data do not state the units of measurement, so each plot shows the weighted mean value as supplied.

What the plots show

Neutrophils are much higher in patients with COVID-19 (about 15.9) than in patients without it (about 3.0), and lymphocytes are lower (about 0.77 compared with 1.00). CRP is slightly higher in patients with COVID-19 (about 81.4 compared with 78.8). Ferritin and troponin are almost the same in both groups.

These plots are descriptive only. No statistical tests were carried out, so the differences should not be described as statistically significant.
